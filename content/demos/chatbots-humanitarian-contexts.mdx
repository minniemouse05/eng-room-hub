# Chatbots in Humanitarian Contexts

Chatbots—automated conversational agents—are increasingly being explored by humanitarian organizations. But deploying AI in contexts where people are vulnerable requires careful consideration. This lesson explores when chatbots might help, when they might harm, and what questions to ask before adopting them.

---

## What Are Chatbots?

A chatbot is software that can have text or voice conversations with humans. Modern chatbots range from simple rule-based systems (following scripts) to sophisticated AI powered by large language models (LLMs) that can understand and generate natural language.

**Types of chatbots:**

| Type | How It Works | Example Use |
|------|--------------|-------------|
| **Rule-based** | Follows pre-written scripts and decision trees | FAQ bots, menu navigation |
| **AI-powered** | Uses machine learning to understand intent | Customer service, triage |
| **LLM-based** | Uses large language models for natural conversation | Information assistance, counseling support |

---

## Why Humanitarian Organizations Consider Chatbots

Organizations explore chatbots for several reasons:

### Potential Benefits

- **Scale:** Reaching more people than human staff alone can serve
- **Availability:** Providing 24/7 access to information
- **Languages:** Supporting multiple languages simultaneously
- **Consistency:** Delivering standardized information reliably
- **Reduced stigma:** Some people feel more comfortable sharing sensitive information with a bot

### Real-World Applications

Chatbots have been piloted for:
- Providing health information in crisis zones
- Answering questions about refugee services
- Mental health support and crisis counseling
- Distributing emergency alerts and safety information
- Collecting feedback from beneficiaries

## The Risks and Tradeoffs

Before getting excited about chatbots, organizations must honestly assess the risks:

### 1. Accuracy and Misinformation

> **Key concern:** Chatbots can give wrong information confidently.

In humanitarian contexts, bad information can be dangerous. If a chatbot gives incorrect health advice, wrong directions to safety, or misleading legal information, real harm results.

**Questions to ask:**
- What happens when the chatbot doesn't know the answer?
- How will we verify the information it provides?
- Who is accountable when errors occur?

### 2. Context and Cultural Sensitivity

AI systems often struggle with:
- Local dialects and informal language
- Cultural nuances and context
- Understanding when someone needs urgent human help
- Recognizing signs of distress or danger

**Example:** A mental health chatbot trained on English data may miss distress signals expressed differently in other cultures.

### 3. Data Privacy and Protection

Using chatbots means collecting conversation data—often sensitive data from vulnerable people.

**Critical questions:**
- Where is conversation data stored?
- Who has access to it?
- Could this data put people at risk if breached?
- Do users understand what happens to their data?
- Are there legal requirements (GDPR, local laws) to consider?

### 4. The Dehumanization Risk

> "Am I talking to a real person who cares, or a machine that processes my words?"

For people in crisis, the human connection may be part of the help itself. Replacing that with automation—even well-intentioned automation—changes the nature of the support.

### 5. Digital Divide

Chatbots assume:
- Access to devices and internet
- Digital literacy
- Ability to type or speak to a device
- Comfort with technology

This can exclude the most marginalized—often the people humanitarian organizations most need to reach.

## Five Key Considerations Before Adoption

Based on research and field experience, here are five essential considerations:

### 1. Is a Chatbot the Right Tool?

Ask honestly: Is this problem best solved by automation?

Sometimes the answer is **no**. If the work requires:
- Deep empathy and human connection
- Complex judgment calls
- High-stakes decisions
- Building trust over time

...a chatbot may not be appropriate.

### 2. What Could Go Wrong?

Conduct a thorough risk assessment:

- **Worst case:** What's the worst outcome if the chatbot fails?
- **Vulnerable users:** How might the most vulnerable users be affected?
- **Failure modes:** How will users know when the bot can't help?
- **Escalation:** Is there a clear path to human support?

### 3. Do You Have the Capacity?

Chatbots are not "set and forget." They require:

- Technical expertise to build and maintain
- Content expertise to ensure accuracy
- Monitoring to catch problems
- Human backup for escalation
- Ongoing training and updates

> **Reality check:** A poorly maintained chatbot is worse than no chatbot.

### 4. Have You Involved Affected Communities?

Before deploying technology that serves a community, involve them in the design:

- What do they actually need?
- How do they prefer to receive information?
- What concerns do they have about AI?
- Have you tested with representative users?

### 5. What Are Your Ethical Red Lines?

Define clear boundaries:

- What will this chatbot never do? (Make medical diagnoses? Give legal advice?)
- When must it always connect to a human?
- What data will never be collected?
- Under what circumstances would you shut it down?

## Case Study: Lessons Learned

A humanitarian organization deployed a chatbot to provide health information during a disease outbreak. Initial results looked promising—thousands of interactions, positive feedback scores.

But deeper analysis revealed:
- The bot struggled with local dialect variations
- Older users often couldn't complete interactions
- Some users shared sensitive symptoms without understanding data wasn't private
- The escalation to human support was rarely used—not because it wasn't needed, but because users didn't know it existed

**The fix:** The organization scaled back the chatbot's role, improved training, added prominent "talk to a human" options, and created clearer data privacy notices.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Chatbots are not neutral tools** - They embed assumptions and carry risks
2. **Humanitarian contexts require extra care** - Vulnerable populations deserve protection
3. **"Can we?" is different from "should we?"** - Capability doesn't equal appropriateness
4. **Human backup is essential** - Always provide paths to human support
5. **Start small and learn** - Pilot carefully before scaling
