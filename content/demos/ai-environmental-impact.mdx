# The Environmental Cost of That AI Query

You ask ChatGPT a question. It answers in seconds. What you don't see: thousands of servers humming in a data center, consuming electricity and water to process your request.

**If your organization works on climate, environment, or sustainability—this is a conversation you need to have about your own AI use.**

## What AI Actually Consumes

Every AI interaction uses three resources most people never think about:

<Callout type="key" title="The hidden ingredients">

**Electricity** — Massive computing power runs the calculations

**Water** — Data centers need cooling; cooling often needs water

**Carbon** — Unless powered by renewables, all that electricity means emissions

</Callout>

### Training vs. Using

AI has two phases, with very different footprints:

| | **Training** | **Using (Inference)** |
|---|---|---|
| **What happens** | AI learns from billions of examples | AI answers your question |
| **When it happens** | Once (or periodically) | Every single query |
| **Energy use** | Enormous one-time cost | Small per query, but adds up |
| **Who pays** | The AI company | You (indirectly), the planet |

<Callout type="example" title="Scale matters">
Training GPT-3 once emitted roughly as much carbon as **five cars driven for their entire lifetimes**.

But billions of queries per day? That inference energy is growing faster than training energy.
</Callout>

### The water you don't see

Data centers get hot. Cooling them often uses water—a lot of it.

- Training GPT-3 used an estimated **700,000 liters of water**
- A large data center can use **3-5 million liters daily**
- Many are located in regions already facing water stress

<Callout type="warning" title="Environmental justice angle">
The communities near data centers bear the water and energy costs. The benefits of AI flow elsewhere. If your organization works on justice issues, this matters.
</Callout>

## Making Better Choices

You're not powerless. Here's how to reduce AI's environmental footprint:

### 1. Ask: Do you actually need AI?

### 2. Right-size your AI

Not all AI is equal. Match the tool to the task:

<ComparisonTable

<Callout type="tip">
**The principle:** Use the simplest AI that solves your problem. Don't use GPT-4 for tasks a basic classifier could handle.
</Callout>

### 3. Choose greener providers

Not all AI providers are equal on sustainability:

**Questions to ask vendors:**

- What powers your data centers? (Renewables? Fossil fuels?)
- Do you offer carbon-neutral computing options?
- Do you publish environmental impact reports?
- Can I choose which region processes my data?

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick win">
Some cloud providers let you select "green regions" powered by renewable energy. The AI works the same; the carbon footprint shrinks.
</Callout>

### 4. Optimize your usage

Once you're using AI:

- **Batch requests** when possible instead of many small queries
- **Cache results** for common questions
- **Set usage limits** to prevent runaway consumption
- **Review periodically** — Do you still need this AI integration?

## Common Pitfalls

<Callout type="pitfall" title="Assuming 'efficiency' means 'green'">
AI is getting more efficient per query. But usage is growing faster than efficiency. The total environmental impact is increasing, not decreasing.
</Callout>

<Callout type="pitfall" title="Ignoring the supply chain">
Your organization's direct AI use is just the start. What about your partners? Your platforms? Your vendors? The AI is everywhere now.
</Callout>

<Callout type="pitfall" title="Waiting for perfect information">
We don't have precise numbers for every AI system's footprint. Don't let that paralyze you. The direction is clear: more AI = more energy. Act on what we know.
</Callout>
