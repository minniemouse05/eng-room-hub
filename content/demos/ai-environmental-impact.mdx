# Environmental Impact of AI

AI systems don't just consume data—they consume enormous amounts of energy and water. As organizations consider adopting AI, understanding these environmental costs is part of responsible decision-making. This lesson breaks down the environmental footprint of AI and what you can do about it.

---

## The Hidden Costs of AI

When you send a message to an AI chatbot, it feels effortless. But behind that simple interaction:

- **Thousands of servers** process your request
- **Massive amounts of electricity** power those servers
- **Cooling systems** prevent equipment from overheating
- **Water** often cools those systems

> Every AI query has a carbon footprint. Most users never see it.

---

## Understanding AI's Energy Consumption

### Training vs. Inference

AI systems use energy in two main phases:

| Phase | What Happens | Energy Impact |
|-------|--------------|---------------|
| **Training** | The AI learns from massive datasets | Enormous one-time cost |
| **Inference** | The AI responds to user queries | Smaller per-query, but adds up |

**Training costs are staggering:**
- Training a large language model can emit as much carbon as **five cars over their entire lifetimes**
- A single training run can cost **millions of dollars** in compute alone
- Training happens once, but the environmental debt is significant

**Inference costs accumulate:**
- Each query uses less energy than training
- But billions of queries per day add up quickly
- As AI becomes more popular, inference energy grows rapidly

### The Numbers

Recent research shows:

- **AI data centers** may consume 3-4% of global electricity by 2030
- **A single ChatGPT query** uses roughly 10x the energy of a Google search
- **Cooling AI systems** can require millions of liters of water daily

---

## Water: The Overlooked Resource

Data centers get hot. Cooling them often requires water.

**How much water?**
- Training GPT-3 consumed an estimated **700,000 liters of water**
- A large data center can use **3-5 million liters per day**
- In water-stressed regions, this creates real tensions with local communities

**Why it matters:**
- Many data centers are located in areas already facing water scarcity
- Climate change is making water resources less predictable
- Local communities may bear costs while benefits flow elsewhere

## Five Things Every Organization Should Know

Based on environmental research, here are five key facts for organizations considering AI:

### 1. Not All AI Is Equal

Different AI approaches have vastly different footprints:

| Approach | Energy Cost | When to Use |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Rule-based systems** | Very low | Simple, predictable tasks |
| **Traditional ML** | Low to moderate | Classification, prediction |
| **Small language models** | Moderate | Specific text tasks |
| **Large language models** | High | Complex reasoning, generation |

> **Principle:** Use the simplest AI that solves your problem.

### 2. Location Matters

Where AI runs affects its carbon footprint:

- Data centers powered by **renewable energy** have lower emissions
- Running AI in regions with **clean grids** reduces impact
- Some cloud providers let you choose **green regions**

**Questions to ask your AI provider:**
- What powers your data centers?
- Do you offer carbon-neutral options?
- Where will my data be processed?

### 3. Efficiency Is Improving (But So Is Demand)

The good news: AI is getting more efficient. Newer models often do more with less.

The challenge: Demand is growing even faster. Efficiency gains are offset by:
- More people using AI
- More complex tasks being automated
- Always-on AI assistants

### 4. "Green AI" Is Emerging

Researchers and companies are working on:

- **Model compression:** Making models smaller without losing capability
- **Efficient architectures:** Designs that use less compute
- **Carbon-aware computing:** Running AI when and where energy is cleanest
- **Renewable-powered data centers:** Shifting to clean energy

### 5. You Have Choices

Organizations aren't powerless. You can:

- Choose providers with strong environmental commitments
- Use AI selectively rather than for everything
- Prefer smaller, task-specific models when appropriate
- Include environmental impact in procurement decisions

## Making Sustainable AI Decisions

Here's a framework for thinking about AI and environment:

### Step 1: Do You Need AI?

Before any AI adoption, ask:
- Is AI necessary, or are simpler solutions available?
- What's the marginal benefit of AI over alternatives?
- Is this a core need or a "nice to have"?

### Step 2: Right-Size Your AI

If AI is appropriate:
- Use the smallest model that meets your needs
- Consider task-specific models over general-purpose ones
- Avoid over-engineering with more AI than necessary

### Step 3: Choose Responsible Providers

When selecting AI services:
- Ask about energy sources and carbon commitments
- Look for transparency in environmental reporting
- Consider providers with science-based targets

### Step 4: Monitor and Optimize

Once using AI:
- Track your usage patterns
- Eliminate unnecessary AI calls
- Cache results where possible
- Review periodically—do you still need this?

## The Bigger Picture

AI's environmental impact exists within larger contexts:

### Environmental Justice

- Data center impacts often fall on local communities
- Water and energy competition affects vulnerable populations
- Benefits of AI may not reach those bearing environmental costs

### Organizational Values

If your organization works on:
- Climate change
- Environmental justice
- Sustainable development

...your AI choices should align with these values.

### Transparency

Consider being open about:
- Your organization's AI usage
- Steps you're taking to minimize impact
- Trade-offs you've made and why

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **AI has real environmental costs** - Energy, water, and carbon emissions
2. **Not all AI is equal** - Different approaches have different footprints
3. **Choices matter** - Providers, models, and usage patterns affect impact
4. **Efficiency helps but isn't enough** - Demand often outpaces improvements
5. **Align AI use with values** - Environmental organizations should practice what they preach
