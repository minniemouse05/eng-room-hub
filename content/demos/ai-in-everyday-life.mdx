# You're Already Using AI

That spam email you never saw? AI caught it. The song that played next on Spotify? AI picked it. The predictive text that finished your sentence? Also AI.

**Most people use AI dozens of times a day without noticing.** Let's make the invisible visible.

## Voice Assistants: Your AI in Plain Sight

"Hey Siri, what's the weather?"
"Alexa, play jazz."
"OK Google, set a timer."

These are the most obvious AI encounters. Voice assistants do three things:

1. **Listen** — They recognize speech and convert it to text
2. **Understand** — They figure out what you're asking for
3. **Act** — They fetch info, control devices, or play media

<Callout type="key">
Voice assistants are designed to feel helpful and human-like. But they're pattern-matching machines, not thinking beings. They work best with clear, simple commands.
</Callout>

<Callout type="example" title="Quick intuition">
Think of a voice assistant like a very fast, very literal personal assistant. Great at looking things up. Not so great at understanding nuance or sarcasm.
</Callout>

## Social Media & Streaming: AI You Don't See

Open Instagram, TikTok, or Facebook. Within seconds, AI has made dozens of decisions about what you'll see first.

**What's happening behind the scenes:**

- **Recommendation engines** rank posts, videos, and ads by what you're most likely to engage with
- **Facial recognition** tags people in photos (often without asking)
- **Content moderation** removes posts that violate rules (sometimes incorrectly)
- **Ad targeting** shows you products based on your behavior

Netflix and Spotify work the same way. That "Top Picks for You" section? An algorithm analyzed your history, compared it to millions of other users, and predicted what you'd enjoy.

<Callout type="warning" title="Watch-out">
Recommendation algorithms optimize for engagement, not your wellbeing. Sometimes the content that keeps you scrolling isn't the content that makes you happier.
</Callout>

## Smart Home & "Hidden" AI

Some AI is easy to spot. Your smart thermostat learns your schedule. Security cameras detect motion.

But most AI hides in plain sight:

| Where | What AI does |
|-------|-------------|
| **Email** | Filters spam, suggests replies, sorts your inbox |
| **Keyboard** | Predicts your next word, corrects typos |
| **Maps** | Calculates routes, predicts traffic |
| **Shopping** | Recommends products, detects fraud |
| **Photos** | Organizes by face, enhances images |
| **Banking** | Flags unusual transactions |

<Callout type="example" title="Example">
When you type "See you at" and your phone suggests "7pm" — that's AI noticing a pattern in your messages.
</Callout>

## What This Means for You

AI being everywhere isn't inherently good or bad. It's a tool. But awareness matters:

**Helpful:**
- Saves time (spam filtering, traffic predictions)
- Makes things easier (voice commands, auto-complete)
- Surfaces relevant content (recommendations that actually fit)

**Worth questioning:**
- Who decides what you see? (algorithmic curation)
- What data is being collected? (privacy tradeoffs)
- Does this serve *you* or the *platform*? (engagement vs. value)

<Callout type="key">
You don't need to fear everyday AI. But understanding where it operates helps you make conscious choices about which conveniences you want—and which you'd rather skip.
</Callout>

## Common Pitfalls

<Callout type="pitfall" title="Thinking 'AI' means 'smart'">
Your voice assistant isn't thinking. It's pattern-matching at high speed. It will confidently give wrong answers because it doesn't understand meaning—just statistics.
</Callout>

<Callout type="pitfall" title="Assuming recommendations are neutral">
Algorithms serve the platform's goals (engagement, purchases). Your feed is designed, not neutral. If something feels addictive, it might be working as intended.
</Callout>

## One Thing People Mix Up

<Callout type="warning" title="Clarification">
**AI ≠ robots.** When people hear "AI," they picture humanoid robots or sci-fi scenes. In everyday life, AI is invisible software: the algorithm picking your next video, the filter catching spam. No robots involved.
</Callout>
