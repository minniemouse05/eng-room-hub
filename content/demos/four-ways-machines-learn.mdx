# Four Ways Machines Learn

There are only four main recipes in the AI cookbook—and once you know them, you'll start seeing them everywhere.

## The Big Picture

Think of AI algorithms as recipes. Each recipe is good at a different kind of task. Some recipes sort things into boxes. Others predict numbers. Others find natural groupings. And one learns by trial and error, like a toddler figuring out how to stack blocks.

Here's the good news: you don't need to memorize formulas. You just need to recognize which recipe fits which situation.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick intuition">
If someone describes an AI system to you, ask yourself: "What's the output?" The answer tells you which recipe they're using.
</Callout>

---

## The Four Recipes

### 1. Classification: The Sorting Hat

This recipe answers yes/no or this/that questions.

**What it does:** Takes an input and assigns it to one of several predefined categories.

**The output is always:** A label or category (spam/not spam, cat/dog, high risk/low risk)

<Callout type="example" title="Real examples">
- **Email:** Spam or not spam?
- **Medical imaging:** Does this scan show signs of disease?
- **Banking:** Is this transaction fraudulent?
- **Support tickets:** Is this billing, technical, or general?
</Callout>

*Quick intuition:* If the answer is a label or category that you defined in advance, it's classification.

---

### 2. Regression: The Number Predictor

This recipe predicts a number on a sliding scale.

**What it does:** Looks at input features and outputs a continuous numerical value.

**The output is always:** A number (price, temperature, score, count)

<Callout type="example" title="Real examples">
- **Real estate:** What will this house sell for?
- **Retail:** How many customers will visit tomorrow?
- **Weather:** What temperature will it be at 3pm?
- **Healthcare:** How many days will this patient stay in hospital?
</Callout>

*Quick intuition:* If the answer is "how much" or "how many," it's regression.

---

### 3. Clustering: The Natural Grouper

This recipe finds patterns you didn't define in advance.

**What it does:** Looks at your data and discovers natural groupings based on similarity.

**The output is always:** Groups or segments that the AI discovered (not ones you told it about)

<Callout type="example" title="Real examples">
- **Marketing:** What types of customers do we have? (Let the AI find the segments)
- **Security:** Which network behaviors are unusual? (Anomaly detection)
- **Research:** What themes emerge from these survey responses?
- **Biology:** How do these genes group together?
</Callout>

*Quick intuition:* If you're asking "what groups exist in this data?" without defining the groups yourself, it's clustering.

---

### 4. Reinforcement Learning: The Trial-and-Error Learner

This recipe learns by doing, getting rewards for good moves and penalties for bad ones.

**What it does:** An "agent" takes actions in an environment, learns from feedback, and gradually figures out the best strategy.

**The output is always:** A policy or strategy (a sequence of decisions)

<Callout type="example" title="Real examples">
- **Games:** Learning to play chess, Go, or video games
- **Robotics:** Teaching a robot arm to pick up objects
- **Recommendations:** Optimizing which content to show in what order
- **Autonomous vehicles:** Learning to navigate safely
</Callout>

*Quick intuition:* If the AI needs to learn a strategy through practice and feedback, it's reinforcement learning.

<Callout type="pitfall" title="One thing people mix up">
**Classification vs. Clustering:** Classification sorts into boxes *you define*. Clustering *discovers* boxes you didn't know existed.

A spam filter is classification (you told it what spam is). Customer segmentation is often clustering (you let it find the segments).
</Callout>

<Callout type="pitfall" title="Another common confusion">
**Regression vs. Classification:** If the output is a category (even if it's numbered, like "risk level 1, 2, or 3"), it's classification. If it's a continuous number (like $247,500), it's regression.
</Callout>

## Test Yourself

<Callout type="checklist" title="Mini-check">
**Question:** A hospital wants to predict how many days a patient will stay. Is this classification, regression, clustering, or reinforcement learning?

**Answer:** Regression—because "how many days" is a number, not a category.
</Callout>
