# How to Evaluate AI Without Getting Burned

Every AI vendor promises transformation. Glossy decks show impressive demos. But six months later, many organizations discover the AI doesn't work as advertised, costs more than expected, or creates problems nobody anticipated.

**This lesson gives you a practical framework to avoid that outcome.**

## First Question: Do You Actually Need AI?

Before comparing vendors, ask the harder question:

<Callout type="key" title="The honest test">
If you can't explain what success looks like without using the word "AI," you're not ready to buy AI.
</Callout>

### When AI makes sense

- You have lots of data and need to find patterns
- The task is repetitive but requires judgment (not just rules)
- Perfect accuracy isn't required—humans can review
- You have capacity to implement *and maintain* it

### When AI probably doesn't make sense

- You're not sure what problem you're solving
- Errors would be severe and irreversible
- You lack quality data
- You need to explain exactly *why* each decision was made
- You don't have ongoing capacity to maintain it

## Evaluating Vendors: Five Dimensions

When you do evaluate AI tools, assess them across these five areas:

### 1. Technical capability

**The core question:** Does it actually work for *your* use case?

<Callout type="warning" title="Demo ≠ Reality">
Demos are designed to impress. They show best-case scenarios with perfect data. Always test with *your* actual data, in *your* actual context.
</Callout>

**Questions to ask:**

- What's the accuracy rate? What kinds of errors does it make?
- Does it work in the languages our communities use?
- Can we pilot before committing?
- What happens when it doesn't know the answer?

**Red flags:**

- Can't provide accuracy metrics
- No trial or pilot option
- Promises that sound too good

### 2. Data practices

**The core question:** What happens to your data?

| Ask this | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Where is data stored? | Jurisdiction affects legal protections |
| Is our data used to train their models? | Your data might benefit competitors |
| Can we delete our data? | Essential for data protection compliance |
| What if we leave? | Data portability matters |

**Red flags:**

- Vague or evasive answers about data
- Data stored in problematic jurisdictions
- Your data improves their product with no benefit to you

### 3. Security and privacy

**The core question:** Can they protect sensitive information?

**Questions to ask:**

- What security certifications do they have? (SOC 2, ISO 27001, etc.)
- How is data encrypted—in transit and at rest?
- Who internally can access our data?
- Have they had breaches? How did they respond?

**Red flags:**

- No recognized security certifications
- Vague answers about encryption
- History of breaches without clear improvements

### 4. Ethics and responsibility

**The core question:** Do they take AI risks seriously?

**Questions to ask:**

- Do they have published AI ethics principles?
- How do they test for and address bias?
- Are they honest about limitations?
- What accountability exists if their AI causes harm?

**Red flags:**

- No ethics principles (or only vague ones)
- Dismissive about bias concerns
- Overpromising capabilities
- No clear accountability

### 5. Support and viability

**The core question:** Will they be a good partner?

**Questions to ask:**

- What training and onboarding do they provide?
- How responsive is support when things go wrong?
- Do they have experience with organizations like ours?
- Are they financially stable?

**Red flags:**

- Poor reviews from similar organizations
- Unresponsive during the sales process (it only gets worse)
- No experience in your sector
- Signs of financial instability

## Checking Your Readiness

Even the best AI tool will fail if your organization isn't ready. Assess yourself honestly:

</Callout>

## The Evaluation Process

Here's a practical timeline:

### Phase 1: Define (2-4 weeks)

- Write a clear problem statement
- Define what success looks like (specific, measurable)
- Identify constraints and requirements
- Get stakeholder alignment

**Output:** Problem brief that everyone agrees on

### Phase 2: Assess readiness (2-4 weeks)

- Use the readiness framework above
- Identify gaps that need addressing
- Decide: proceed, pause, or stop

**Output:** Readiness assessment with action plan

### Phase 3: Research (2-4 weeks)

- Identify potential vendors/tools
- Gather initial information
- Create shortlist (3-5 options)

**Output:** Shortlist with initial scores

### Phase 4: Deep evaluation (4-8 weeks)

- Request detailed information
- Check references (especially from similar organizations)
- Conduct pilots with your actual data
- Score against your criteria

**Output:** Evaluation matrix

### Phase 5: Decide (1-2 weeks)

- Review with stakeholders
- Make selection—or decide not to proceed
- Document your rationale

**Output:** Decision document

### Phase 6: Implement and monitor (ongoing)

- Execute carefully
- Monitor against expectations
- Be willing to adjust or exit

## Common Pitfalls

<Callout type="pitfall" title="Falling for the demo">
Demos are theater. The AI is shown with perfect data, ideal conditions, and cherry-picked examples. Always, always pilot with your own data.
</Callout>

<Callout type="pitfall" title="Ignoring total cost">
License fees are just the start. Real costs include:
- Implementation and integration
- Training your team
- Ongoing maintenance and updates
- Staff time for oversight
- Opportunity cost of what you're not doing instead

Budget 2-3x the license cost for the first year.
</Callout>

<Callout type="pitfall" title="Underestimating change">
AI changes how people work. Expect resistance, confusion, and a learning curve. Plan for change management from day one.
</Callout>

<Callout type="pitfall" title="Skipping the ethics review">
Ethical problems often emerge after deployment. By then, you've invested resources and reputation. Include ethics review in your evaluation—not as an afterthought.
</Callout>

<Callout type="pitfall" title="No exit strategy">
Before you start, know how you'd stop. What happens to your data? How do you migrate to something else? Plan your exit before your entry.
</Callout>
