# Evaluating AI Solutions for Your Organization

AI vendors promise transformation. Marketing materials glow with possibilities. But how do you separate genuine value from hype? This lesson provides practical frameworks for evaluating AI solutions—from assessing vendors to checking your own organizational readiness.

---

## Before You Evaluate: Is AI the Right Tool?

Before diving into vendor comparisons, pause and ask:

### The Fundamental Question

> "Is AI the best solution to this problem, or are we reaching for AI because it's trendy?"

Sometimes the answer is:
- **A simpler tool would work better** (spreadsheet, database, rule-based automation)
- **A human process is more appropriate** (relationship-building, complex judgment)
- **The problem isn't well-defined enough** (AI needs clear goals to work toward)
- **The data doesn't exist** (AI needs data to learn from)

### When AI Might Be Right

AI tends to work well when:
- You have large amounts of data
- The task is repetitive but requires pattern recognition
- Perfect accuracy isn't required (humans can review)
- The problem is well-defined with clear success metrics
- You have capacity to implement and maintain it

### When AI Might Not Be Right

Be cautious if:
- You're not sure what problem you're solving
- The consequences of errors are severe and irreversible
- You lack data or your data is poor quality
- You need to explain exactly why decisions are made
- You don't have ongoing capacity to maintain the system

## Framework 1: Evaluating AI Service Providers

When assessing AI vendors or tools, consider these dimensions:

### Technical Capability

| Question | Why It Matters |
|----------|----------------|
| Does it actually work for your use case? | Marketing claims vs. reality |
| How accurate is it? What are the error rates? | Understanding limitations |
| Does it handle your languages and contexts? | Many AI tools are English-centric |
| Can you test before committing? | Pilots reveal real-world performance |

**Red flags:**
- Vendor can't provide accuracy metrics
- No trial or pilot option
- Promises sound too good to be true

### Data Practices

| Question | Why It Matters |
|----------|----------------|
| What data does the system need? | Understanding data requirements |
| Where is data stored and processed? | Privacy and jurisdiction |
| Is your data used to train their models? | Your data may benefit competitors |
| What happens to data if you stop using the service? | Data portability and deletion |

**Red flags:**
- Unclear data policies
- Data stored in problematic jurisdictions
- No option to delete your data
- Your data improves their product without compensation

### Security and Privacy

| Question | Why It Matters |
|----------|----------------|
| What security certifications do they have? | Evidence of security practices |
| How is data encrypted? | Protection in transit and at rest |
| Who can access your data? | Internal access controls |
| Have they had security breaches? | Track record matters |

**Red flags:**
- No security certifications
- Vague answers about encryption
- History of breaches without improvement
- Resistance to security audits

### Ethics and Responsibility

| Question | Why It Matters |
|----------|----------------|
| Do they have published AI ethics principles? | Stated commitments |
| How do they handle bias? | Awareness of fairness issues |
| What's their transparency about AI limitations? | Honesty about capabilities |
| Do they have responsible AI governance? | Structured accountability |

**Red flags:**
- No ethics principles or they're vague
- Dismissive of bias concerns
- Overpromising and underdelivering
- No clear accountability for AI harms

### Support and Partnership

| Question | Why It Matters |
|----------|----------------|
| What training and onboarding do they provide? | Getting your team up to speed |
| How responsive is technical support? | When things go wrong |
| Do they have experience with organizations like yours? | Relevant expertise |
| What's their roadmap and stability? | Long-term viability |

**Red flags:**
- Poor reviews from similar organizations
- Unresponsive during sales process
- No experience in your sector
- Financial instability

## Framework 2: Organizational Readiness Assessment

Before adopting AI, honestly assess whether your organization is ready:

### Data Readiness

| Dimension | Questions |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Data availability** | Do you have the data AI needs? Is it accessible? |
| **Data quality** | Is your data accurate, complete, and consistent? |
| **Data governance** | Do you have policies for data management? |
| **Data privacy** | Can you use this data ethically and legally? |

**Action items if not ready:**
- Audit existing data
- Establish data quality processes
- Create data governance policies
- Review privacy obligations

### Technical Infrastructure

| Dimension | Questions |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Integration** | Can AI tools connect with your existing systems? |
| **Computing resources** | Do you have necessary hardware/cloud access? |
| **Technical staff** | Do you have people who can implement and maintain AI? |
| **Security infrastructure** | Can you protect AI systems adequately? |

**Action items if not ready:**
- Map integration requirements
- Budget for infrastructure
- Hire or train technical capacity
- Strengthen security practices

### Organizational Capacity

| Dimension | Questions |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Leadership buy-in** | Do decision-makers understand and support AI adoption? |
| **Change management** | Can your organization adapt to new ways of working? |
| **Staff readiness** | Are employees prepared to work with AI? |
| **Resources** | Do you have budget for implementation and ongoing costs? |

**Action items if not ready:**
- Educate leadership on AI realities
- Develop change management plan
- Invest in staff training
- Create realistic budget (including ongoing costs)

### Governance Readiness

| Dimension | Questions |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Policies** | Do you have AI use policies? |
| **Accountability** | Who is responsible for AI decisions and outcomes? |
| **Monitoring** | How will you track AI performance and problems? |
| **Ethics oversight** | Who reviews AI decisions for ethical concerns? |

**Action items if not ready:**
- Draft AI use policies
- Assign clear accountability
- Establish monitoring processes
- Create ethics review mechanisms

## The Evaluation Process

Here's a practical process for evaluating AI solutions:

### Step 1: Define the Problem (2-4 weeks)

- Write a clear problem statement
- Define what success looks like
- Identify constraints and requirements
- Get stakeholder alignment

**Deliverable:** Problem brief that everyone agrees on

### Step 2: Assess Readiness (2-4 weeks)

- Evaluate organizational readiness using framework above
- Identify gaps that need addressing
- Decide whether to proceed or pause

**Deliverable:** Readiness assessment with action plan

### Step 3: Research Options (2-4 weeks)

- Identify potential vendors/tools
- Gather initial information
- Create shortlist (3-5 options)

**Deliverable:** Shortlist with initial assessment

### Step 4: Deep Evaluation (4-8 weeks)

- Request detailed information from shortlisted vendors
- Check references from similar organizations
- Conduct demos or pilots
- Score against your criteria

**Deliverable:** Evaluation matrix with scores and notes

### Step 5: Decide (1-2 weeks)

- Review evaluation with stakeholders
- Make selection or decide not to proceed
- Document decision rationale

**Deliverable:** Decision document

### Step 6: Implement and Monitor (ongoing)

- Execute implementation plan
- Monitor performance against expectations
- Adjust or exit if needed

**Deliverable:** Operational AI system with monitoring

## Common Evaluation Mistakes

Learn from others' errors:

### Mistake 1: Falling for the Demo

Demos are designed to impress. Real-world performance is often different.

**Fix:** Always pilot with your actual data and use cases.

### Mistake 2: Ignoring Total Cost

License fees are just the start. Real costs include implementation, training, maintenance, and opportunity costs.

**Fix:** Calculate total cost of ownership over 3-5 years.

### Mistake 3: Underestimating Change

AI changes how people work. Resistance and adjustment take time.

**Fix:** Plan for change management from the start.

### Mistake 4: Skipping Ethics Review

Ethical problems often emerge after deployment, causing reputation damage and harm.

**Fix:** Include ethics review in evaluation process.

### Mistake 5: No Exit Strategy

If the AI doesn't work, can you stop using it? What happens to your data?

**Fix:** Plan for exit before you start.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Start with the problem** - Is AI actually the right solution?
2. **Assess your readiness** - Technical, organizational, and governance capacity matters
3. **Evaluate vendors systematically** - Use frameworks to compare fairly
4. **Include ethics and responsibility** - Not optional considerations
5. **Plan for the long term** - Implementation and maintenance, not just purchase
