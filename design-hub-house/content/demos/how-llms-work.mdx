# How LLMs Work

Ever wondered what actually happens when you ask ChatGPT or Claude a question? Let's demystify it - no computer science degree required!

---

## The Core Idea: Prediction

Here's the surprising truth: **at their heart, LLMs are prediction machines.**

They've been trained to answer one simple question over and over:

> "Given these words, what word is most likely to come next?"

That's it! But this simple capability, applied at massive scale with sophisticated techniques, leads to remarkably intelligent-seeming behavior.

---

## A Simple Example

Let's say you type: **"The capital of Kenya is"**

The LLM looks at those words and predicts what comes next:

| Possible Next Word | Probability |
|-------------------|-------------|
| **Nairobi** | 95% |
| located | 2% |
| a | 1% |
| the | 0.5% |
| ... | ... |

It picks "Nairobi" because that's what it learned from reading millions of texts where this pattern appeared.

> **Key insight:** The LLM doesn't "know" facts like a database. It has learned patterns about what words tend to follow other words.

---

## The Building Blocks

Let's understand the key components that make LLMs work:

### 1. Tokens: Breaking Text into Pieces

LLMs don't read words the way we do. They break text into **tokens** - which might be whole words, parts of words, or even single characters.

**Example:**

```sh
"transformative" → "trans" + "form" + "ative"
"AI" → "AI" (single token)
"don't" → "don" + "'" + "t"
```

**Why does this matter?**

- LLMs process tokens, not words
- Longer responses = more tokens = more processing
- This is why some services charge "per token"

---

### 2. Context Window: The Model's Memory

The **context window** is how much text the LLM can "see" at once when generating a response.

Think of it like this:

> **Analogy:** Imagine reading a book, but you can only see the current page and the previous 10 pages. That's your "context window."

| Model Type | Typical Context Window |
|------------|----------------------|
| Older models | ~4,000 tokens (a few pages) |
| Modern models | 100,000+ tokens (a small book) |

**Why does this matter?**

- Longer context = can handle longer documents
- But the model can "forget" things from the beginning if the conversation gets too long

---

### 3. Attention: Finding What Matters

The **attention mechanism** is the secret sauce that made modern LLMs possible.

When processing your question, the model doesn't treat all words equally. It learns to pay more **attention** to the words that matter most for the current task.

**Example:** In the sentence "The bank by the river had beautiful flowers"

When figuring out what "bank" means, the model pays special attention to "river" - which tells it we're talking about a riverbank, not a financial institution.

> **This is revolutionary:** Before attention mechanisms, AI struggled with context. Now it can understand that the same word means different things in different situations.

---

### 4. Parameters: The Brain's Settings

**Parameters** are the millions (or billions!) of numerical values that control how the model processes information.

Think of them like:

- Settings on a massive mixing board
- Each one fine-tuned during training
- Together they encode everything the model has learned

| Model | Parameters |
|-------|-----------|
| Small model | ~100 million |
| GPT-3 | 175 billion |
| Latest models | 1+ trillion |

More parameters generally = more capability (but also more computing power needed).

---

## The Generation Process

When you ask an LLM a question, here's what happens:

```ini
1. Your text is broken into tokens
         ↓
2. Tokens are converted to numbers the model understands
         ↓
3. The model processes these through many layers
         ↓
4. Attention helps focus on relevant parts
         ↓
5. The model predicts the most likely next token
         ↓
6. That token is added, and steps 3-5 repeat
         ↓
7. This continues until the response is complete
```

**The key point:** The model generates text **one token at a time**, each time considering everything that came before.

## Why This Matters for You

Understanding how LLMs work helps you:

### Use Them Better

- **Be specific:** More context = better predictions
- **Break down complex tasks:** Easier predictions = better results
- **Understand limitations:** They predict, they don't truly "know"

### Spot Problems

- If the model seems to lose track, the context window might be full
- If it makes up facts, remember: it's predicting likely text, not consulting a database

### Have Realistic Expectations

- They're incredibly capable pattern matchers
- They're not conscious or truly understanding
- They work best as tools to augment human work

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **LLMs predict the next word** - over and over, very well
2. **Tokens are the building blocks** - not quite words, not quite letters
3. **Context windows limit memory** - they can only "see" so much at once
4. **Attention enables understanding** - helping the model focus on what matters
5. **Parameters encode learning** - billions of settings fine-tuned on vast data
