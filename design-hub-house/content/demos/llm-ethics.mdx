# Ethical Considerations for LLMs

With great power comes great responsibility. LLMs are incredibly capable tools, but using them thoughtfully requires understanding the ethical landscape. This guide will help you navigate these waters.

---

## Why Ethics Matter for AI

You might think: "I'm just using a chatbot - why do ethics matter?"

Here's why:
- **What you create affects others** - AI-generated content reaches real people
- **Biases can cause harm** - Unfair or misleading outputs have consequences
- **Trust is fragile** - Organizations that misuse AI lose credibility
- **The stakes are rising** - As AI becomes more powerful, so do the risks

---

## Key Ethical Dimensions

### 1. Bias and Fairness

**The challenge:** LLMs can reflect and amplify biases present in their training data.

**What this looks like:**
- Stereotyping certain groups of people
- Providing different quality responses based on names or cultural references
- Reinforcing historical inequalities present in text data

**Example:**
> An LLM asked to write a story about a doctor might default to male pronouns, while defaulting to female pronouns for nurses - reflecting biases in its training data.

**What you can do:**
- Review outputs for biased language or assumptions
- Explicitly request inclusive content when appropriate
- Be aware that "neutral" isn't always truly neutral

---

### 2. Transparency and Disclosure

**The challenge:** People have a right to know when they're interacting with AI or reading AI-generated content.

**Key questions:**
- Should you disclose when content is AI-generated?
- How should AI-assisted work be credited?
- What transparency do stakeholders expect?

**Best practices:**
- Be upfront about AI use in your workflows
- Disclose when published content was AI-generated or AI-assisted
- Don't try to pass off AI writing as entirely human-written

> **Principle:** When in doubt, disclose. Transparency builds trust.

---

### 3. Misinformation and Harmful Content

**The challenge:** LLMs can generate convincing false information, and this can spread quickly.

**Risks include:**
- Hallucinated "facts" being published as truth
- AI-generated misinformation on important topics
- Convincing fake content (sometimes called "deepfakes" for text)

**What you can do:**
- Verify all factual claims before publishing
- Don't use AI to generate deceptive content
- Be especially careful with health, legal, and financial information

---

### 4. Impact on Workers

**The challenge:** AI changes the nature of work, affecting knowledge workers, writers, and creative professionals.

**Considerations:**
- How does AI assistance change job roles?
- What skills become more or less valuable?
- How do we ensure AI augments rather than replaces human work?

**A balanced perspective:**
- AI can handle routine tasks, freeing humans for higher-value work
- But transitions need to be managed thoughtfully
- Workers deserve support in adapting to AI-augmented workflows

---

### 5. Access and Equity

**The challenge:** AI benefits shouldn't flow only to those with resources and technical knowledge.

**Issues to consider:**
- Advanced AI tools often require payment
- Technical expertise affects how well people can use AI
- Language barriers limit access (many models work best in English)
- Infrastructure requirements exclude some communities

**What organizations can do:**
- Consider how AI initiatives might affect equity
- Look for ways to make AI benefits accessible
- Be mindful of who might be left behind

---

## Responsible Use Principles

Here are five principles to guide ethical AI use:

### 1. Human Oversight

**Keep humans in the loop for important decisions.**

- AI should assist, not replace, human judgment
- Critical decisions need human review
- Someone should be accountable for AI-influenced outcomes

### 2. Purpose Limitation

**Use AI for appropriate applications with clear boundaries.**

- Define what you will and won't use AI for
- Be cautious about expanding AI into sensitive areas
- Just because you *can* use AI doesn't mean you *should*

### 3. Disclosure

**Be transparent about AI use.**

- Tell stakeholders when AI is being used
- Label AI-generated content appropriately
- Don't hide or obscure AI involvement

### 4. Feedback Mechanisms

**Create channels to identify and address problems.**

- Make it easy to report AI errors or harmful outputs
- Actively seek feedback from affected communities
- Have processes to respond to concerns

### 5. Continuous Evaluation

**Regularly assess impacts and adjust.**

- Monitor how AI use affects your work and stakeholders
- Be willing to change course if problems emerge
- Stay informed about evolving best practices

## A Framework for Civil Society Organizations

If your organization is considering AI adoption, ask these four questions:

### Question 1: Who might be harmed?

> "Who might be harmed or excluded if we adopt this AI solution?"

Consider:
- Direct users of the AI system
- People affected by AI-influenced decisions
- Communities represented (or misrepresented) in outputs
- Workers whose roles might change

### Question 2: What transparency do we owe?

> "What transparency do we owe to stakeholders about our AI use?"

Consider:
- What do funders, partners, and beneficiaries expect?
- What disclosures are appropriate for your context?
- How will you communicate about AI use?

### Question 3: How do we maintain human agency?

> "How do we maintain human agency and decision-making?"

Consider:
- Which decisions should never be automated?
- What review processes are needed?
- How do we prevent over-reliance on AI?

### Question 4: How do we verify accuracy?

> "How can we verify AI outputs and ensure accuracy?"

Consider:
- What fact-checking processes are needed?
- Who is qualified to review AI outputs?
- What are the consequences of errors?

## Practical Tips

### For Content Creation
- Always review and edit AI-generated text
- Verify facts, names, dates, and statistics
- Ensure the tone and message align with your values
- Disclose AI assistance as appropriate

### For Research and Analysis
- Use AI as a starting point, not a final source
- Cross-reference important findings
- Be aware of potential biases in AI summaries
- Document how AI was used in your methodology

### For Organizational Decisions
- Don't let AI make decisions about people without human review
- Ensure affected parties have recourse if AI causes problems
- Keep humans accountable for AI-influenced outcomes

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Bias is real** - AI can reflect and amplify societal biases
2. **Transparency builds trust** - Disclose AI use appropriately
3. **Verification is essential** - Don't trust AI outputs blindly
4. **Human oversight matters** - Keep people in the loop
5. **Think about impact** - Consider who benefits and who might be harmed
